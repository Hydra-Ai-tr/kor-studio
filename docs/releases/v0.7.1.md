Kor Studio 0.7.1 trains text models fully on the graphics card: up to 17 times faster than the CPU on a laptop.

**Intro video:** [English](https://github.com/Hydra-Ai-tr/kor-studio/releases/download/v0.7.1/kor-studio-video-en.mp4) · [Türkçe](https://github.com/Hydra-Ai-tr/kor-studio/releases/download/v0.7.1/kor-studio-video-tr.mp4)

## New

- **Whole training step on the GPU.** With *Train on the graphics card* in the training settings (`--gpu` on the command line), a text model runs embeddings, layer norms, attention, the MLP, dropout, the loss and all backward passes through OpenCL. The activations stay on the card; only the weights go in and the gradients come back, and the optimizer stays on the CPU. Version 0.7.0 sent only the large matrix products, which gained little because every product crossed the PCIe bus.
- **Checked on your data.** At the start one step of your data runs on both the CPU and the GPU with dropout off. If the loss or the gradients disagree, training stays on the CPU and the log says why. A model too large for the GPU memory sends only its attention and large products to the card; LoRA fine-tuning and table models do the same.
- **`kor-cli gpu` times a whole step.** It checks every kernel against the CPU (attention against a double-precision version, dropout and loss weights against finite differences) and prints the speed of one training step on both.

One training step, RTX 3050 Ti Laptop GPU against all 8 threads of an i5-12500H (AVX2), same loss and gradients within 2e-6:

| Text model | CPU step | GPU step | Speed-up |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.17M parameters | 95.7 ms | 26.3 ms | 3.6x |
| 0.83M parameters | 350.6 ms | 49.8 ms | 7.0x |
| 4.85M parameters | 2144.8 ms | 127.1 ms | 16.9x |

Larger models gain more; other cards and CPUs will give other numbers.

## Download

- `kor-studio-0.7.1-setup.exe`: the installer.
- `kor-studio-0.7.1-windows-x64.zip`: the portable version; extract it and run `kor-studio.exe`.
- `kor-studio-0.7.1-linux-x64.tar.gz`: Linux.

Windows 10 or newer with a 64-bit CPU is required. Python 3 is only needed for plugins and skills. The programs are not code-signed yet, so Windows SmartScreen may ask for confirmation (*More info* → *Run anyway*).

## Known limitations

- Image and sound models are transformers too and take the same GPU path, checked against the CPU in the same way, but the speed-ups above were measured on text models only. Table models send only their large matrix products to the card.
- Tested on an NVIDIA laptop GPU. AMD and Intel cards with OpenCL should work and are checked against the CPU before training, but are untested.
- The releases are not code-signed. On PCs with Smart App Control turned on, Windows may refuse to run them.
